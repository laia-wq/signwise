# Signwise demo walkthrough

Suggested 60–90 second recording using a real camera and your own hand:

1. Introduce Signwise as an experimental ASL alphabet practice app. Show the A–Z reference library.
2. Enter a short name such as “Lily.” Explain that this is deliberate letter-by-letter practice, not fluent word recognition.
3. Enable the camera and show the brief framing check. Demonstrate one correction: change thumb position and point out the amber highlight and written hint.
4. Hold a accepted match, then lower the hand to advance. Show the name progress strip filling in.
5. Start a five-letter test. Explain that hints are hidden and timeouts count as incorrect. After finishing, show “Practice missed letters” and the focused retest.
6. Close with privacy and scope: processing happens on-device; no camera video or typed name is uploaded. Recognition is heuristic and has not been independently validated. Link the GitHub repository.

Do not fabricate a successful camera match or present synthetic tests as real-world accuracy. If recognition fails while recording, describe the limitation or use a clearly disclosed edited demonstration. Do not include other people or private surroundings without their consent.

## Real-signer evaluation (not yet performed)

Ask willing ASL-fluent signers, ideally including a Deaf educator, to review the reference instructions and attempt all 26 letters. Get consent before recording anything; recording is not needed to note results.

For each letter, record manually: device/browser, hand used, approximate camera angle and lighting, whether the signer considers the pose correct, whether the app accepted it, how long acceptance took, and which correction appeared. Include deliberate wrong-thumb and lookalike poses. Cover repeated name letters, both hands, J/Z motion, camera denial, and mobile framing.

Summarize false acceptance and false rejection separately. Keep the demo labelled experimental until this evaluation has been completed; do not invent an accuracy percentage.
